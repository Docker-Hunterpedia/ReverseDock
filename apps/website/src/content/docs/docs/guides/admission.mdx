The `admission` middleware <Status planned="v0.9" /> keeps a backend in the range where it
answers quickly. It learns how many requests the backend can have **in flight** from how its
latency reacts, and answers the rest with **503 Service Unavailable** (problem type
[`overloaded`](/docs/reference/problems/#overloaded)) and `Retry-After`, before they reach
the backend. Routes declare which requests go first: under overload, `sheddable` routes are
refused before `normal` ones, and `critical` routes last. It is off unless you configure it.

```yaml title="rdock.yaml"
listeners:
  web: { address: ":80" }
defaults: { listeners: [web], tls: off }
middlewares:
  shed:
    type: admission
    mode: aimd
    min: 4
    max: 200
    latency: 300ms      # a slower answer counts as a sign of overload
routes:
  checkout:
    match: "Host(`shop.example.com`) && PathPrefix(`/checkout`)"
    service: shop
    middlewares: [shed]
    priority_class: critical
  reports:
    match: "Host(`shop.example.com`) && PathPrefix(`/reports`)"
    service: shop
    middlewares: [shed]
    priority_class: sheddable
  pages:
    match: "Host(`shop.example.com`)"
    service: shop
    middlewares: [shed]
services:
  shop:
    backends: [{ address: "127.0.0.1:3000" }]
```

Every route that names the same `admission` middleware shares **one** limit. Use one middleware
per backend pool, and put it before the middlewares that do work (it costs two atomic
operations per request, and takes no lock).

## How the limit adapts

`mode` is required and picks the algorithm; both come from published designs (read on
2026-11-13):

- **`aimd`**, Netflix concurrency-limits' `AIMDLimit`
  ([source](https://github.com/Netflix/concurrency-limits)): an answer slower than `latency`,
  or a `502`, `503` or `504` (the backend failed or timed out), multiplies the limit by
  `backoff_percent`; any other answer adds one while at least half of the limit is in use.
  Simple and predictable; you choose the latency you accept.
- **`gradient`**: the gradient of Envoy's adaptive concurrency filter
  ([docs](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/configuration/http/http_filters/adaptive_concurrency_filter))
  with the smoothing of Netflix's `Gradient2Limit`: the limit follows
  `tolerance × no-load latency ÷ latency`, clamped to 0.5–1, plus `queue`, smoothed. The
  no-load latency is measured, as in Envoy: at start and every `window` answers, the limit is
  held at `min` until `probe` requests admitted at that concurrency have finished, and their
  median becomes the reference. Requests above `min` get `503` meanwhile; Envoy documents the
  same effect. Nothing to tune for a backend whose latency is unknown.

Latency is measured from admission to the response headers. The limit starts at `initial` and
starts again from there after a reload.

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `mode` | required | `aimd` or `gradient`. |
| `min` | `3` | The lowest limit (Envoy's `min_concurrency`). |
| `max` | `1000` | The highest limit (Envoy's `max_concurrency_limit`); at most 100000. |
| `initial` | `20` | The limit at start (Netflix's), kept between `min` and `max`. |
| `latency` | `5s` | `aimd`: answers slower than this lower the limit (Netflix's `timeout`). |
| `backoff_percent` | `90` | `aimd`: the factor applied on a slow or failed answer, 50–99. |
| `tolerance_percent` | `150` | `gradient`: how much slower than the no-load latency an answer may be, 100–1000 (Netflix's `rttTolerance`). |
| `smoothing_percent` | `20` | `gradient`: weight of a new estimate, 1–100. |
| `queue` | `4` | `gradient`: requests allowed above the estimate (Netflix's `queueSize`). |
| `window` | `600` | `gradient`: answers between two measurements of the no-load latency, 10–100000. |
| `probe` | `50` | `gradient`: answers that make one measurement, 5–1000 (Envoy's `request_count`). |
| `shares.normal` | `90` | Percent of the limit `normal` routes may fill. |
| `shares.sheddable` | `75` | Percent of the limit `sheddable` routes may fill; at most `shares.normal`. |
| `retry_after` | `1s` | `Retry-After` of a refused request. |

A setting of the other algorithm is refused (`latency` with `mode: gradient`, `window` with
`mode: aimd`, …), as are `min` above `max`, an `initial` outside them and `sheddable` above
`normal`.

## Priority classes

`priority_class` is a route setting: `critical`, `normal` (the default) or `sheddable`. It is
unrelated to the route's `priority`, which only orders matching.

- A `sheddable` request is admitted while the requests in flight are below
  `shares.sheddable` of the limit, a `normal` one below `shares.normal`, a `critical` one below
  the whole limit.
- `critical` and `normal` requests also keep a **reserve** of their own: the part of the limit
  between their share and the next lower one (with the defaults, 10 % and 15 %). When the
  limit falls below what lower classes already hold, those requests drain first and the reserve
  keeps critical requests going meanwhile. So the requests in flight stay below the limit once
  it is steady, and below twice the limit at worst while it falls.

A `priority_class` on a route without an `admission` middleware has no effect; validation
warns.

## Seeing it work

- Metrics: `rdock_admission_limit{middleware}`, `rdock_admission_in_flight{middleware}` and
  `rdock_admission_shed_total{middleware,class}` ([metrics](/docs/reference/metrics/)).
- The access log has `admission: "shed:<class>"` on refused requests.

## What to know

- **Per instance.** Each instance learns its own limit; there is no cluster-wide admission.
- **One per route.** Two `admission` middlewares on one route are refused, and so is an
  `in_flight` with `key: route` next to one: two caps on the same requests would hide the
  latency the adaptive one learns from. `in_flight` per client (`key: client_ip`, `consumer`,
  `group`, …) is fine beside it.
- **No queue.** Requests over the limit are answered at once; clients should honour
  `Retry-After`. Health checks, the admin API and ACME challenges never pass through it.
- **Samples are not all used.** An answer that finishes while another one updates the limit is
  skipped rather than waited for, so no request waits on another.
