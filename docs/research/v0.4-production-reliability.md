# v0.4 research: production reliability

Research note for milestone **v0.4 "Production reliability"**. Requirements in scope: M-LB-01…10, M-TLS-01, M-SEC-01, M-RT-02…07, M-TM-01…07, M-OBS-01…03, M-L4-05, W-CF-07, W-CF-08, H-ENG-01…03. Every fact was checked on 2026-10-29 against the source linked next to it; Pingora facts against the published 0.9.0 crate sources (`pingora-proxy`, `pingora-core`, `pingora-load-balancing`, `pingora-ketama`). The decisions that follow are in [ADR-0022](../decisions/0022-production-reliability.md).

## 1. What Pingora 0.9 gives us

- **Retry loop** (`pingora-proxy/src/lib.rs`, `proxy_to_upstream` loop): every attempt calls `upstream_peer` again, so each retry can pick another backend. The loop stops when the error is not retryable or after `ServerConf.max_retries` (default 16; ReverseDock sets 2 today). The cap is per proxy, not per request: per-service attempt limits and budgets must be our own counters in the request context, enforced with `set_retry(false)`.
- **Hooks for health and retries:** `fail_to_connect(session, peer, ctx, e)` on connect errors, which are created **not** retryable (`pingora-error`, `Error::create`), so a hook must `set_retry(true)` to try another backend; `error_while_proxy` after upstream errors before response headers (the reuse-only retry state panics if read before `decide_reuse`, [ADR-0015](../decisions/0015-status-code-retries.md)); `upstream_response_filter` sees the status (the peer comes from our context), and a retryable error returned there enters the retry loop; `connected_to_upstream` sees the peer, reuse and connection timing; `logging` runs once per request.
- **Request body replay:** retry buffering is always on, with a fixed, non-configurable 64 KiB limit (`protocols/http/v1/common.rs` `BODY_BUF_LIMIT`, `v2/server.rs`); larger bodies mark the buffer truncated (`retry_buffer_truncated()`), and such requests cannot be retried.
- **Upstream mTLS under rustls:** `HttpPeer.client_cert_key: Option<Arc<CertKey>>` and `HttpPeer::new_mtls`; the connector calls `with_client_auth_cert` per handshake, and the key is part of the pool key (`upstreams/peer.rs`, `connectors/tls/rustls/mod.rs`). `CertKey::new` asserts a non-empty chain and the connector unwraps the key conversion, so the PEM must be parsed and checked when the configuration loads.
- **Connection pool:** one LRU of `upstream_keepalive_pool_size` (default 128) idle connections per `HttpProxy`, grouped by peer; idle eviction by `PeerOptions.idle_timeout`. There is **no per-peer connection limit**; one needs our own counter.
- **Load balancing:** `pingora-load-balancing` (the `lb` feature) has round-robin, random, FNV hash and ketama, TCP/HTTP health checks and a background discovery service. It is built around its own discovery, not per-reload snapshots, and ketama ignores Unix sockets. `pingora-ketama` 0.9.0 alone (`Continuum`, `Bucket`) is a small crate; `Bucket::new` asserts a non-zero weight.
- **Client certificates:** our listener builds the rustls `ServerConfig` ([ADR-0012](../decisions/0012-own-listeners-pingora-http-engine.md)), today with `with_no_client_auth()`; `WebPkiClientVerifier::builder_with_provider(roots, ring)` with `allow_unauthenticated()` gives "optional". The verified chain is available from the TLS connection (`peer_cert_chain_der`) in our handshake callback and reaches the request through the digest extension, as the SNI already does. Pingora's own `get_organization_serial_bytes` calls `expect` on x509 parsing of a peer certificate (`utils/tls/rustls.rs`), so our callback must not rely on it for client certificates.
- **Compression:** `pingora_core::protocols::http::compression` encodes gzip, brotli, zstd and Dcz; brotli and zstd are already in `Cargo.lock` through pingora-core.
- **gRPC-Web:** `pingora_core::modules::http::grpc_web` converts gRPC-Web to gRPC.
- **Mirroring:** nothing built in. Subrequests exist (`allow_spawning_subrequest`), but a fire-and-forget request through our own client, as custom error pages do, is simpler and bounded.
- **Timing:** no per-request upstream latency; `TimingDigest` covers connection setup only. Latency must be measured with our own `Instant`s.

## 3. How others do it

- **Retry budgets:** Envoy `retry_budget` defaults to 20 % of active requests with at least 3 concurrent retries; circuit-breaker `max_retries` 3; backoff fully jittered from 25 ms, capped at 250 ms; `gateway-error` = 502/503/504 ([router filter](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/configuration/http/http_filters/router_filter), [circuit_breaker.proto](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/api-v3/config/cluster/v3/circuit_breaker.proto)). Linkerd allows 20 % plus 10 retries per second ([retries](https://linkerd.io/2.15/tasks/configuring-retries/)); gRPC throttles with a token bucket ([A6](https://github.com/grpc/proposal/blob/master/A6-client-retries.md)). Traefik v3's retry middleware retries listed status codes, and never non-idempotent methods unless told to ([retry](https://doc.traefik.io/traefik/reference/routing-configuration/http/middlewares/retry/)).
- **Health checks:** Traefik v3 active checks default to 30 s interval and 5 s timeout, `mode: http|grpc`, 2xx/3xx healthy, plus passive checks (`failureWindow` 10 s, `maxFailedAttempts` 1) ([service](https://doc.traefik.io/traefik/reference/routing-configuration/http/load-balancing/service/)). HAProxy checks every 2 s, rise 2, fall 3 ([health checks](https://www.haproxy.com/documentation/haproxy-configuration-tutorials/reliability/health-checks/)). Envoy outlier detection ejects after 5 consecutive 5xx for 30 s, growing to 300 s, at most 10 % of hosts ([outlier_detection.proto](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/api-v3/config/cluster/v3/outlier_detection.proto)); below 50 % healthy hosts it sends to all ("panic", [panic threshold](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/intro/arch_overview/upstream/load_balancing/panic_threshold)). NGINX never marks the only server of an upstream down ([upstream](https://nginx.org/en/docs/http/ngx_http_upstream_module.html)). Caddy's passive checks are off by default ([reverse_proxy](https://caddyserver.com/docs/caddyfile/directives/reverse_proxy)).
- **Circuit breakers:** Envoy caps connections, pending and active requests at 1024 each by default; Traefik's `circuitBreaker` opens on an expression (`NetworkErrorRatio()`, `ResponseCodeRatio(…)`, `LatencyAtQuantileMS(…)`), checks every 100 ms, stays open 10 s and recovers over 10 s ([circuitbreaker](https://doc.traefik.io/traefik/reference/routing-configuration/http/middlewares/circuitbreaker/)).
- **Balancing:** Traefik v3 offers `wrr`, `p2c`, `hrw` (consistent hashing on client IP) and `leasttime`; its sticky cookie holds a SHA-256 hash of the server URL, unsigned, and is not `Secure`/`HttpOnly` by default ([service](https://doc.traefik.io/traefik/reference/routing-configuration/http/load-balancing/service/), [traefik#11518](https://github.com/traefik/traefik/pull/11518)). Caddy's cookie policy is an HMAC-SHA256 of the upstream with a secret ([reverse_proxy](https://caddyserver.com/docs/caddyfile/directives/reverse_proxy)). Envoy's least-request picks 2 random hosts and weights by `w / (active + 1)` ([load balancers](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/intro/arch_overview/upstream/load_balancing/load_balancers)); bounded-load consistent hashing is [Mirrokni et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.01350).
- **Limits:** Traefik `inFlightReq` answers 429 ([inflightreq](https://doc.traefik.io/traefik/reference/routing-configuration/http/middlewares/inflightreq/)); Envoy's local rate limit answers 429 with optional `Retry-After` ([local rate limit](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/configuration/http/http_filters/local_rate_limit_filter)); its overload manager answers 503 ([overload manager](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/configuration/operations/overload_manager/overload_manager)). `Retry-After` is defined for 503 and 429 ([RFC 9110 §10.2.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-retry-after), [RFC 6585 §4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6585.html#section-4)). The RateLimit header fields are still a draft (`draft-ietf-httpapi-ratelimit-headers-11`, 2026-05-23, [datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-httpapi-ratelimit-headers/)).
- **mTLS:** Traefik's `clientAuthType` has five modes, two of which accept certificates without verifying them ([TLS options](https://doc.traefik.io/traefik/reference/routing-configuration/http/tls/tls-options/)); its `passTLSClientCert` sets `X-Forwarded-Tls-Client-Cert` (PEM) and `-Info` ([passtlsclientcert](https://doc.traefik.io/traefik/reference/routing-configuration/http/middlewares/passtlsclientcert/)). Envoy's `x-forwarded-client-cert` (XFCC) carries `Hash`, `Subject`, `URI`, `DNS`, and is stripped from clients by default ([headers](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/configuration/http/http_conn_man/headers)). Caddy defaults to `require_and_verify` when a trust pool is set ([tls](https://caddyserver.com/docs/caddyfile/directives/tls)).
- **Cloudflare Authenticated Origin Pulls** ([AOP](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/origin-configuration/authenticated-origin-pull/)): global, zone-level and per-hostname. The global certificate is shared by every Cloudflare account, and Cloudflare advises zone-level or per-hostname AOP with your own certificate for stricter security ([global](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/origin-configuration/authenticated-origin-pull/set-up/global/)). Its CA ([PEM](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/static/authenticated_origin_pull_ca.pem), `CN=origin-pull.cloudflare.net`) is valid until 2029-11-01.
- **JWT:** [RFC 8725](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8725.html): the verifier fixes the allowed algorithms (§3.1), never `none`; checks `iss` (§3.8) and `aud` (§3.9); does not follow `jku`/`x5u` (§3.10). OIDC Core allows a small clock leeway and re-fetches the JWKS on an unknown `kid` ([OIDC Core §10.1.1](https://openid.net/specs/openid-connect-core-1_0.html)). Envoy's `jwt_authn` caches remote JWKS for 10 minutes, allows 60 s of clock skew, strips the token unless `forward`, and clears `claim_to_headers` headers before verification ([jwt_authn](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/api-v3/extensions/filters/http/jwt_authn/v3/config.proto)).
- **Tracing:** W3C Trace Context Level 1 is a Recommendation ([trace-context](https://www.w3.org/TR/trace-context/)). OTel HTTP span conventions are stable: server span name `{method} {http.route}`, attributes `http.request.method`, `url.path`, `url.scheme`, `http.response.status_code`, `http.route`; client spans carry `http.request.resend_count` on retries; 5xx is an error ([http-spans](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/http/http-spans/)). OTLP/HTTP defaults to port 4318 and `/v1/traces` ([exporter](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/otel/protocol/exporter/)); the default sampler is `parentbased_always_on` ([SDK env vars](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/otel/configuration/sdk-environment-variables/)).
- **Cloudflare Load Balancing monitors** ([monitors](https://developers.cloudflare.com/load-balancing/monitors/), [create monitor](https://developers.cloudflare.com/load-balancing/monitors/create-monitor/)): GET by default, a fixed `Cloudflare-Traffic-Manager` User-Agent, expected codes such as `2xx`, the endpoint's `Host` header.

## 5. Open points for the implementation

- Pingora's `max_retries` must be at least the largest configured attempt count; set it from the configuration (it is per `HttpProxy`, so a listener change rebuilds it) or fix it at 16 and limit per request.
- Health state must survive reloads: keep it in a registry keyed by service and address, shared by generations like the DNS cells in `rdock-proxy/src/upstream.rs`.
- Bodies over 64 KiB cannot be retried or mirrored; say so in the docs and the access log.
